03. Disk Geometry & Arm Scheduling

For over five decades, magnetic Hard Disk Drives (HDDs) served as the primary secondary storage substrate in computer systems. While modern operating systems increasingly boot from solid-state flash memory, understanding mechanical disk geometry and head scheduling remains essential for studying physical latency modeling, mechanical movement optimization, and sequential data locality.

1. Physical Disk Geometry: Platters, Cylinders, and Sectors

A magnetic disk drive is a precision electro-mechanical device composed of several circular rigid platters stacked along a central rotating spindle.

Figure 2.1: Physical Geometry of a Magnetic Hard Disk Drive
How stacked magnetic platters, read/write heads, concentric tracks, and cylinders organize data storage.

From CHS to Logical Block Addressing (LBA)

In early computers, operating systems had to know the exact physical geometry:

  • CHS Addressing: Every request specified (Cylinder, Head, Sector). The OS told the drive: "Move to Cylinder 102, activate Head 3, read Sector 14."
  • Zoned Bit Recording (ZBR): Outer tracks have a much larger circumference than inner tracks. Modern drives pack up to twice as many sectors on outer tracks as inner tracks. CHS assumed fixed sector counts per track, making physical addressing obsolete.
  • Logical Block Addressing (LBA): The disk controller abstracts geometry completely. The drive presents a simple linear array of logical blocks indexed from $0$ to N - 1: 
    LBA 0, LBA 1, LBA 2, ..., LBA 976,773,167
    The on-board disk firmware maps each LBA address to internal physical cylinder, head, and track locations, transparently managing bad sector remapping and track skewing.

2. Modeling I/O Access Latency (TI/O)

Reading or writing a block of data on a mechanical disk drive incurs four distinct physical delays:

The Fundamental Disk Access Latency Equation: 
TI/O = Tseek + Trotational + Ttransfer + Tcontroller
  • Seek Time (Tseek): The time required for the voice-coil actuator arm to accelerate, travel mechanically across platters, decelerate, and settle precisely over the target cylinder. Average seek time is typically 4 to 9 milliseconds.
  • Rotational Latency (Trotational): Once the head settles on the track, it must wait for the target sector to spin under the read head. On average, the disk must rotate half a revolution: 
    Trotational (avg) = 1/2 × (60 / RPM)
    At 7,200 RPM, one revolution takes 8.33 ms → Trotational (avg) = 4.17 ms.
    At 15,000 RPM, one revolution takes 4.00 ms → Trotational (avg) = 2.00 ms.
  • Transfer Time (Ttransfer): The time to read the actual data bits off the platter as it spins underneath: 
    Ttransfer = Byte Count / Internal Track Transfer Rate
    For a 4 KB block at 200 MB/s, Ttransfer ≈ 0.02 ms (negligible compared to seek and rotation!).
  • Controller Overhead (Tcontroller): Microprocessor setup, DMA bus command, and ECC verification (< 0.01 ms).

The Crucial Architectural Takeaway: Seek time and rotational delay account for over 95% to 99% of total I/O latency. Random accesses run at ∼150 IOPS (∼10 ms per read), while sequential accesses stream at 200 MB/s. Operating system disk schedulers exist to minimize mechanical seek distances!

3. Disk Arm Scheduling Algorithms

When multiple processes issue disk requests concurrently, the operating system kernel queues pending requests. The Disk Arm Scheduler decides the order in which pending cylinder requests are serviced to minimize mechanical arm travel.

The Classical Test Workload

Consider a disk with cylinders numbered 0 to 199. The disk head is currently located at cylinder 53. The pending request queue holds 8 cylinder accesses:

Algorithm Analysis & Comparisons

Algorithm Head Movement (Workload) Core Behavioral Characteristic Primary Defect / Limitation
FCFS 640 Cylinders Completely fair, zero starvation. Wild head thrashing; slowest throughput.
SSTF 236 Cylinders Greedy local minimum; fast response under low loads. Pathological Starvation of outer tracks.
SCAN (Elevator) 208 Cylinders Sweeps end-to-end; eliminates starvation completely. Unequal waiting distribution for recent cylinders.
C-LOOK 322 Cylinders Single-direction sweep with fast return; uniform wait times. Slightly higher total head movement than SCAN.
