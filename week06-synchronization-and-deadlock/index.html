Week 6: Synchronization & Deadlock

Now that our operating system supports true multiprocessing and preemptive scheduling, we must confront the pathologies of concurrent execution. Expanding beyond race conditions on shared memory, we examine the systemic failure states that emerge when threads compete for scarce system resources: Deadlock, Livelock, and Starvation.

📚 What You Will Learn
  • Differentiating Deadlock (permanent circular blockage), Livelock (active state oscillation without forward progress), and Starvation (scheduling unfairness).
  • The Four Coffman Conditions governing necessary and sufficient deadlock states.
  • Resource Allocation Graphs (RAGs), cycle detection in single-unit resources, and reduction algorithms.
  • The four canonical handling strategies: The Ostrich Algorithm, Deadlock Prevention, Deadlock Detection and Recovery, and Deadlock Avoidance via Dijkstra's Banker's Algorithm.
  • Classical synchronization challenges: Dining Philosophers, Readers-Writers, and Producer-Consumer defenses in Linux and Windows kernels.
